Anshun Posubao

chinesecookingdemystified.substack.com

Simultaneously fluffy and flaky, Posubao is our all time favorite bao. This is a version from Anshun in the Guizhou province, which uses fermented rice to catalyze a starter dough instead of yeast

Laozao Fermented Rice

  • 250g long grain sticky rice
  • 1.8g yeast ball

Bean Paste

  • 250g rice beans or red adzuki beans

To Boil

  • 1000ml water
  • 0.5g sodium carbonate

To Fry

  • 100ml neutral oil
  • 60g dark brown sugar

Sugar and Perilla Mixture

  • 6g granulated sugar
  • 6g toasted perilla seed

Pork Filling

  • 150g pork leg or belly
  • 20g sliced scallions

Seasoning

  • 1.5g salt
  • 2g sugar
  • 2g cornstarch
  • 0.5g white pepper powder
  • 0.5g sichuan peppercorn powder
  • 2g shaoxing wine
  • 1g light soy sauce
  • 15g water
  • 5g oil

Starter

  • 50g active laozao fermented rice
  • 50g all purpose flour
  • 25g water

Sponge Dough

  • 60g starter
  • 300g all purpose flour
  • 135g water

Main Dough and Final Wrapping

  • sponge dough from above
  • 300g all purpose flour
  • 135g water
  • 30g sugar
  • 1g sodium carbonate

Laozao Fermented Rice

  1. Soak 250g long grain sticky rice overnight.
  2. Strain and transfer to a steamer lined with wet cloth. Steam for 45 minutes, splashing ~240ml water every 15 minutes to prevent drying.
  3. Cool to 35°C.
  4. Dissolve 1.8g yeast ball in a bit of cool drinking water, mix with steamed rice.
  5. Cover and ferment in a warm (30°C), dark place for 72 hours.

Bean Paste

  1. Soak 250g rice beans or red adzuki beans overnight.
  2. Next day, boil beans in 1000ml water + 0.5g sodium carbonate over medium-low flame for ~1 hour until very soft.
  3. Rinse to remove shells using strainer and cloth bag setup. Squeeze out moisture.
  4. Fry paste with 100ml neutral oil and 60g dark brown sugar over low flame for ~30 minutes until thick and dark.

Sugar and Perilla

  1. Toast 6g perilla seeds in a dry pan over medium heat for ~2 minutes until fragrant.
  2. Pound toasted seeds with 6g granulated sugar into coarse powder.

Pork Filling

  1. Mince 150g pork leg or 60–70% lean pork belly.
  2. (If using belly, cube fat and mince lean separately.)
  3. Mix with: 1.5g salt, 2g sugar, 2g cornstarch, 0.5g white pepper powder, 0.5g Sichuan peppercorn powder, 2g Shaoxing wine, 1g light soy sauce, 15g water
  4. Stir until sticky.
  5. Add 20g sliced scallions and any pork fat. Mix.
  6. Coat with 5g oil and set aside.

Starter (Laomian)

  1. Mash 50g active laozao (25g rice + 25g liquid).
  2. Mix with 50g all-purpose flour and 25g water.
  3. Cover and ferment in warm, dark place for 12+ hours until bubbly.

Sponge Dough

  1. Mix 60g starter with 135g water.
  2. Add 300g all-purpose flour.
  3. Knead into ball, press down, cover, and ferment overnight.

Main Dough

  1. Dissolve 30g sugar in 135g water.
  2. Mix 1g sodium carbonate into 300g all-purpose flour.
  3. Combine sugar water, sponge dough, and flour.
  4. Transfer to surface and knead 12 minutes until white and smooth.
  5. Divide into 3 pieces (~320g each). Shape into logs. Rest 30 minutes covered.

Dough Rolling

  1. Divide one log into 2 equal pieces.
  2. Roll one piece into a 20cm x 70cm sheet, ~1mm thick.
  3. Spread 15g lard evenly over surface. Roll tightly into log.
  4. Roll second piece the same way, spread another 15g lard.
  5. Wrap first log inside second sheet and roll again.
  6. Wrap in ziplock, rest 30 minutes. Repeat with remaining logs.

Filling and Wrapping

  1. Cut rested log into 4 equal pieces (~80g each).
  2. Press into disc, fill each with: 15g pork filling, 7g bean paste, 2.5g sugar-perilla powder
  3. Pleat into bao shape. Ragged pleats are OK.
  4. Place each on parchment in steamer. Repeat for remaining dough.

Proofing and Steaming

  1. Place steamer over 35°C water. Proof 30 minutes.
  2. Steam over boiling water on high heat for 13 minutes.
  3. Turn off heat, rest 1 minute before opening.

To maintain starter:

Every 2 weeks, bring to room temp overnight. Discard liquid layer.

Mix in fresh flour and water (1:1 ratio), let rise 12 hours until doubled or tripled.

Use or refrigerate.

Homemade Sodium Carbonate

Spread baking soda on a plate.

Bake at 150°C for 1 hour.

Cool and store in airtight jar for future use.
