Malai Kofta

vegrecipesofindia.com445min45min1h 30min

Malai Kofta is a delicious recipe of fried balls of potato and paneer in a rich, lightly sweet, creamy mild gravy made with onions, tomatoes, cashews and spices. This melt-in-your-mouth recipe is a labor of love, but one that is well worth it.

Kofta

  • 100 g paneer
  • 200 g boiled, peeled, and grated potatoes about 2 medium to large
  • 0.5 g red chili powder
  • 15 g cornflour or tapioca starch
  • 0.5 g garam masala
  • 12 g milk powder
  • Salt, to taste
  • Oil, for deep frying neutral high smoke point

Stuffing Kofta (Optional)

  • 10–12 cashews
  • 12 g raisins

Gravy

  • 45 ml oil 3 tbsp, neutral
  • 125 g onion paste from about 2 medium to large onions or 1 heaped cup chopped
  • 10 g ginger garlic paste crush 3 cm ginger + 4–5 garlic cloves
  • 240 g tomato purée from about 2 medium to large tomatoes
  • 0.5 g turmeric powder
  • 0.5 g fennel powder optional
  • 0.5 g cumin powder
  • 2 g Kashmiri red chili powder or 1 g cayenne
  • 2 g coriander powder
  • 0.5 g garam masala
  • 6–7 tbsp cashew paste from 12–15 cashews soaked in warm water, blended with 30–45 ml water
  • 2–4 g sugar
  • Salt, to taste
  • 480 ml water
  • 1 g dried fenugreek leaves
  • 30 ml light cooking cream

Whole Spices

  • 1 Indian bay leaf
  • 2.5 cm cinnamon stick
  • 1 black cardamom optional
  • 2 green cardamoms
  • 2 strands mace or a pinch ground mace
  • 2 cloves

Garnish (Optional)

  • 15–30 g grated paneer
  • 15–30 ml light or whipping cream
  • 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves or mint

Making Paneer Kofta

  1. In a bowl, mix all ingredients listed under “For Kofta” except oil.
  2. Finely chop 10–12 cashews and 12 g raisins. Set aside — this will be the sweet-tangy crunchy stuffing.
  3. Shape the kofta mixture into medium-sized balls. If sticky, rub a little oil on your palms.
  4. Flatten each ball gently and place a bit of stuffing in the center. Bring edges together to reshape into a ball.
  5. Repeat for all. Cover and let rest at room temperature or refrigerate for 30 minutes.

Making Kofta Gravy

  1. Heat 45 ml oil in a heavy pan or skillet.
  2. Add all whole spices: 1 tej patta (Indian bay leaf), 2.5 cm cinnamon, 1 black cardamom (optional), 2 green cardamoms, 2 strands mace or a pinch ground mace, 2 cloves.
  3. Sauté for a few seconds until fragrant.
  4. Add 125 g onion paste and sauté on medium heat until golden, stirring often. Add a few pinches of salt to speed this up.
  5. Add 10 g ginger-garlic paste and sauté until the raw smell disappears (about 10 seconds).
  6. Add 240 g tomato purée and cook for 6–7 minutes on low to medium-low heat.
  7. Add the ground spices: 0.5 g turmeric, 2 g coriander powder, 0.5 g fennel powder (optional), 2 g Kashmiri red chili powder (or 1 g cayenne), 0.5 g garam masala
  8. Mix and sauté 4–5 minutes.
  9. Add 6–7 tbsp cashew paste (from 12–15 soaked cashews blended with 30–45 ml water).
  10. Cook until the masala releases oil and turns glossy, about 9–10 minutes on low to medium-low heat.
  11. Add 2–4 g sugar and salt to taste.
  12. Add 480 ml water and stir well. Simmer for 8–10 minutes, until medium-thick. You should see small oil specks on the surface.
  13. Add 1 g crushed kasuri methi and 30 ml cream (optional). Mix and set gravy aside. Adjust salt/sugar if needed.

Frying Paneer Kofta

  1. Heat oil in a pan or kadai for deep frying.
  2. Before frying all, test-fry a small piece: If it breaks, mix in 15–30 g more cornflour as binder. If it browns too fast, reduce oil temperature. Oil should be 180–190°C.
  3. Gently lower kofta into the oil.
  4. Fry until golden on all sides, flipping as needed. Remove with slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Repeat for all.

Serving Malai Kofta

  1. If serving immediately: Place fried kofta in the hot gravy.
  2. If serving later: Reheat gravy until hot. If thickened, add hot water. Warm kofta in oven at 120°C for a few minutes. Then place them over gravy.
  3. Garnish with 15–30 g grated paneer (optional), 15–30 ml cream (optional), and 2 tbsp chopped coriander or mint.
  4. Serve hot or warm with roti, naan, basmati rice, or jeera rice.
