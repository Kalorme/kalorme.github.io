Guichai Tod

hot-thai-kitchen.com4-515 min45 min1 hour

A popular Thai street food, these garlic chive dumplings are chewy in the middle and crispy on the outside, and the chives just melt in your mouth. Easy to make, and can be done ahead of time as well!

Dumplings

  • 230 g garlic chives
  • 8 g sugar
  • 8 ml soy sauce
  • 3 g salt
  • 1 g baking soda optional
  • 10 ml neutral oil
  • 1 garlic clove, finely grated or minced or 1 g garlic powder
  • 0.5 g white pepper
  • 100 g rice flour
  • 100 g tapioca starch
  • 240 ml water

Sauce

  • 15 g sugar
  • 15 ml water
  • 15 ml vinegar
  • 25 ml sweet soy sauce kecap manis
  • 15 ml soy sauce
  • 10–15 ml sambal oelek or minced Thai chilies to taste
  1. Cut about 4 cm off the bottom of the garlic chives and discard. Some garlic chives can harbor dirt between the leaves near the base—if so, shake them in a bowl of cold water to remove it.

After washing, dry the chives thoroughly!

  1. Excess water will make the dough mushy. Shake off water as much as possible (a salad spinner works well), then lay the chives on a clean tea towel, cover with another, and gently roll them between the two towels to dry.
  2. Once washed and dried, chop the chives into small pieces (just under 1 cm) and place them in a mixing bowl. Add 3 g salt, 8 g sugar, 8 ml soy sauce, 1 g baking soda (optional), 0.5 g white pepper, 10 ml neutral oil, and 1 finely grated garlic clove (or 1 g garlic powder). Mix until combined. Let sit for at least 20 minutes, or until wilted and liquid has been drawn out.

Make the dipping sauce

  1. Combine 15 ml vinegar, 15 ml water, and 15 g sugar. Stir until the sugar dissolves. Add 25 ml sweet soy sauce (kecap manis), 15 ml soy sauce, and 10–15 ml sambal oelek or minced Thai chilies. Stir to combine. This sauce will keep for weeks in the fridge.

Make the batter

  1. In a pot, combine 240 ml water, 100 g rice flour, and 100 g tapioca starch. Stir until no lumps remain. Turn the heat on low and stir constantly with a spatula. As the mixture begins to thicken and form lumps, remove the pot from heat occasionally and mash any lumps against the side. Keep stirring constantly until the batter reaches a thick, yogurt-like consistency that can suspend the chives evenly. Remove from heat and stir well to break up any remaining lumps.
  2. Off heat, stir in the chive mixture until fully combined.
  3. Grease a 18 cm round cake pan (or similar heatproof container) with oil and line the bottom with parchment paper. Pour in the batter and smooth the top.
  4. Preheat a steamer with boiling water. If you’re not using a bamboo lid, wrap a towel around the lid to prevent water from dripping onto the dumplings. Alternatively, cover the pan with foil. Steam over rapidly boiling water for 15–20 minutes or until fully cooked (internal temperature should reach at least 88°C). Cooking time may vary depending on the container size and batter thickness.
  5. Let the dumpling cake cool completely, or chill until ready to serve (easier to cut when cold). You can store it for up to 1 week. When ready to eat, run a knife along the edges, flip it out, and cut into bite-sized pieces with a sharp oiled knife. Wipe and re-oil the blade as needed to avoid sticking.

Fry the dumplings

  1. Add just enough oil to a non-stick pan to reach just below halfway up the dumpling pieces. Heat until hot, then fry the dumplings on both sides until browned and crispy. Make sure they don’t touch each other in the pan or they’ll stick together. Drain on paper towels.
  2. Serve hot and crispy with the dipping sauce!
